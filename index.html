Seminar 1 — Ancient Britain · Roman Britain · Anglo-Saxon England

  • Ancient Britain
    • Settlement: hunter-gatherers; land bridge; Boxgrove ~500k; Kents Cavern Neanderthal 35–40k; modern humans 9–12.5k
    • Climate: warming ~12k; Channel formed ~6.5k; Wildwood forest
    • Neolithic: forest clearance; 4 → 25 people/sq mi; soil exhaustion ~2500 BC
    • Security: hill forts (Maiden Castle); symbolic forts (Silbury); Stonehenge/Avebury 3000–1500 BC
    • Metals: Bronze 2100–700 BC; Iron ~500 BC; 3000+ hill forts
    • Literacy: Pytheas 325 BC; Celts oral tradition
  • Roman Britain
    • Conquest: Caesar 55/54 BC; Claudius AD 43; Medway; Colchester; 11 kings surrender
    • Resistance: Wales (Silures, Ordovices); Boudicca AD 60 (Colchester, St Albans, London)
    • Expansion: Agricola 77–83; Mons Graupius 83; withdrawal 86
    • Frontiers: Hadrian’s Wall 122–126; Antonine Wall 142–143 (abandoned 163); Saxon Shore
    • Decline: Carausius 286–296; Great Raid 367; last troops 407; Honorius 410
    • Life: grid towns; forum/basilica/baths; villas (mosaics, hypocaust); roads; Druids destroyed; Christianity by 2nd c.
  • Anglo-Saxon England
    • Invasion: ~450 Angles/Saxons/Jutes; Mount Badon ~520; Deorham 577
    • Christianity: Augustine 597; Canterbury 601; Whitby 664
    • Vikings: Lindisfarne 793; Danes 865; Ashdown 871; Edington 878; Wedmore 879 (Danelaw)
    • Reconquest: Edward the Elder; Brunanburh 924–939; Danelaw absorbed
    • Society: thanes → churls → thralls; ~1 mln; villages self-sufficient; ~10% towns by 11th c.
    • Economy: 8-ox plough; 2–3 field; wheat/barley/rye; subsistence; famines
    • Place names: -ham, -tun/-ton, -hurst, -bury, -chester/-caster/-cester
    • Tribes: Picts (Alba), Scots (Gaels+Picts), Saxons (ealdormen, Marklo), Angles (England), Jutes (Kent, Hants, Wight)
    • Alfred: burhs; half-army system; Athelney; Edington 878; Guthrum baptised; law/schools/translations
    • Edward the Confessor: 1043 crowned; Magnus threat; Godwine conflict 1051; Westminster Abbey 1065; died 1066

Seminar 2 — Norman England · Domesday Book · Plantagenets · Magna Carta

  • Norman Conquest
    • 1066: Harold king; Hardrada Stamford Bridge; Hastings; William crowned
    • William: illegitimate; Duke 1035; Val-es-Dunes 1047; Matilda 1053; Mortemer 1054; propaganda claim
    • Control: Harrying of the North 1069–70; New Forest 1074; Domesday 1086
    • Succession: Rufus 1087; killed 1100; Henry I; White Ship 1120; Stephen vs Matilda 1135–54
  • Domesday Book (1086)
    • Structure: Little Domesday (Norfolk/Suffolk/Essex); Great Domesday; 13,418 places; omissions (London, Winchester, northern counties)
    • Order: king → church → lay tenants-in-chief → serjeants/thegns
    • Purpose: geld inquest; feudal rights; mercenary burden; order; possibly William II
    • Records: owner 1066; owner 1086; transfer; value; peasants (villani, bordars)
    • Transfers: antecession; castleries; forfeiture; aggression; marriage
    • Limits: human error; Roman numerals; omissions; manor concept unclear
    • Terms: hundred; hide (40–120 acres); vill; demesne; virgate; TRE
  • Plantagenets & Magna Carta
    • Timeline: Henry II 1154; Becket 1170; Richard 1189; John 1199; Liverpool 1207; Magna Carta 1215; Henry III 1216; Lewes 1264; Evesham 1265; Edward I 1272; Jews expelled 1290; Model Parliament 1295; Bannockburn 1314; Hundred Years War 1337
    • Magna Carta: 63 clauses; 3 still law; church; London; clause 39 (lawful judgement); legacy — king subject to law; parchment + Great Seal
    • De Montfort’s Parliament 1265: elected reps; knights + burgesses; first “parliament”
    • Caxton: c.1422–1492; Bruges; Cologne; Westminster press 1476; 100+ books; Chaucer, Gower, Malory

Seminar 3 — Tudor England

  • Tudor Timeline
    • Henry VII: 1485; Cabot 1497
    • Henry VIII: 1509; Catherine; Mary Rose 1511; Flodden 1513; divorce 1533; Supremacy 1534; Pilgrimage of Grace 1536; monasteries 1536–40; 6 wives; 1547 dies
    • Edward VI: 1547; Somerset; Prayer Book 1549; Northumberland; dies 1553
    • Mary I: 1553; Jane Grey 1554; ~300 burned 1555–58; dies 1558
    • Elizabeth I: 1558; excommunicated 1570; Mary QoS 1587; Armada 1588; dies 1603
    • Society: Great Rebuilding c.1560–1640; East India Co 1600; Poor Law 1601
  • Society & Everyday Life
    • Population: 2.5 mln (15th c.) → 3 mln (1525) → 4 mln (1600); inflation; real wages fall; famine 1594–97
    • Classes: nobility → gentry/merchants → yeomen/craftsmen → tenant farmers/labourers; ~50% subsistence
    • Vagrancy: 1530 licences; 1536 whipping/ear; 1547 slavery; 1572 branding; 1576 workhouses; 1597 death penalty abolished
    • Housing: half-timbered; brick; thatch/tiles; glass windows (lattice); chimneys; oak panelling; four-posters; rushes; beeswax/tallow
    • Food: rich meat-heavy; poor bread/cheese/onions; fish days; white vs coarse bread; honey; new foods (turkey 1525, potato 1580s, tomato, apricot); ale/beer/wine
    • Towns: London 60–70k → 250k; Bristol 14k → 20k; Norwich 10k → <20k; York 10k → 12k; dirty, no sewers; plague
    • Games: tournaments; hunting; hawking; wrestling; casting bar; billiards; chess; backgammon; tennis; bowls; cards; football (rough); cockfighting; bear baiting; executions
    • Clothes: wool (fine/coarse); linen; silk/cotton (rich); breeches, doublets, jerkins; smock, bodice, skirt; farthingale/bum roll; hats/coif; sumptuary laws
  • Religious Documents
    • Act of Supremacy 1534: king = Supreme Head; oath; annulment; monastery wealth; Treasons Act; repealed 1554; restored 1559 (Elizabeth = Supreme Governor; Oath; penalties)
    • Act of Six Articles 1539: transubstantiation; cup; celibacy; chastity vows; private masses; confession; “bloody whip with six strings”; repealed under Edward VI
  • Mary, Queen of Scots
    • Early: 1542 Linlithgow; father dies 6 days later; crowned 1543; Rough Wooing 1544–51; France
    • Marriages: Francis (2 yrs); Darnley 1565; James VI/I; Bothwell; Darnley murdered
    • End: fled to England; imprisoned 19 yrs; beheaded Fotheringhay 1587

Seminar 4 — England in the 17th Century

  • Political Timeline
    • Early Stuart: James I 1603; Gunpowder 1605; Jamestown 1607; KJV 1611; Charles I 1625; Petition of Right 1628; Eleven Years Tyranny 1629–40; Laud 1633
    • Civil War: Grand Remonstrance 1641; Edgehill 1642; Marston Moor 1644; Naseby 1645; Charles surrenders 1646; Preston 1648; Pride’s Purge; beheaded 1649
    • Commonwealth: Worcester 1651; Hobbes Leviathan; Dutch War 1652–54; Cromwell Lord Protector 1653; Major-Generals 1655–57; dies 1658; Richard resigns 1659
    • Restoration: Charles II 1660; Royal Society 1662; turnpike 1663; Plague 1665; Fire 1666; Milton Paradise Lost 1667; Habeas Corpus 1679; James II 1685; Glorious Revolution 1688; Bill of Rights 1689; Bank of England 1694; steam engine 1698
  • Life & Society
    • Population: 4 mln (1600) → 5.5 mln (1700); trade/commerce grow; London 500k+; colonies; banking; goldsmiths → Bank of England
    • Classes: nobility → gentry → yeomen → craftsmen/tenant farmers/labourers; 1601 Poor Law overseers
    • London: west end affluent / east end poor; piped water; 1680s street lighting; plague 1603, 1636, 1665
    • Housing: oak → walnut/mahogany; veneer/inlay/lacquer; chests of drawers; grandfather clocks; bookcases; upholstered chairs; Inigo Jones classical; brick/stone; glass cheaper; sash windows
    • Food: forks; bananas/pineapples; tea/coffee; coffee houses; ice cream; poor: bread/cheese/onions/pottage
    • Clothes: ruffs → lace collars; breeches; doublet → waistcoat/frock coat; wigs; women: shift, bodice, skirt; face patches
    • Leisure: cards/bowls/tennis; chess/draughts/backgammon; pall-mall; yachting; theatre (banned 1642, restored 1660); cockfighting/bear baiting; first newspaper 1621
    • Education: petty school; grammar school (boys); tutors; boarding schools for girls (accomplishments); corporal punishment
    • Science: Bacon — observation + experiment; nature as machine; “Divine Watchmaker”; Royal Society 1662; Wren; Milton; Purcell; witchcraft decline (last execution 1685)
  • Key Documents
    • Petition of Right 1628: no taxes without Parliament; no imprisonment without cause; no quartering; no martial law in peace
    • Habeas Corpus Act 1679: challenge unlawful imprisonment; criminal charges; suspendable in unrest; £500 penalty; 2-year limit
    • Bill of Rights 1689: no Catholic monarch; petition; no suspending laws; no taxation without Parliament; free elections; free speech in Parliament; no standing army in peace; no excessive fines/cruel punishments; frequent Parliaments
  • Oliver Cromwell
    • Early: gentry; Cambridge; farming/rents; modest income; MP 1640
    • Military: captain 1642 → colonel 1643; Eastern Association; Marston Moor 1644; New Model Army; Naseby 1645; Preston 1648; Ireland 1649–50; Scotland 1650–51; Worcester 1651
    • Political: regicide 1649; Lord Protector 1653; tolerant civilian regime; foreign policy; died 1658

Seminar 5 — England in the 18th & 19th Centuries

  • 18th-Century Timeline
    • Succession: Act of Settlement 1701; Anne 1702; Union 1707; George I 1714
    • War: Blenheim 1704; Ramillies 1706; Oudenarde 1708; Malplaquet 1709; Seven Years 1756–63; Quebec 1759; American independence 1783

    • Society: Defoe 1719; South Sea Bubble 1720; Walpole 1721; 10 Downing St 1735; Turpin 1739; Wesley 1739; Gordon Riots 1780; convicts to Australia 1787
  • 18th-Century Life
    • Population: 6.5 mln → 9 mln (1801); land = wealth; middle class grows; ~50% subsistence
    • Towns: Improvement Commissioners; paving/cleaning/lighting; industrial towns; London ~1 mln
    • Housing: country houses; Capability Brown; Robert Adam neo-classical; rich upholstered furniture; poor 1–3 rooms
    • Clothes: breeches, stockings, waistcoats, frock coats; wigs; tricorn hats; women: stays, hooped petticoats; no knickers; fans
    • Leisure: chess/draughts/backgammon; horse racing (Jockey Club 1727, Derby 1780); theatre; assembly rooms; cricket (Hambledon ~1750); spas; seaside; novels; circulating libraries; The Times 1785; cruel sports; Grand Tour
    • Education: charity/Blue Coat schools; grammar schools; girls — accomplishments; dissenting academies
  • 18th-Century Documents
    • Act of Settlement 1701: Protestant succession; Sophia → George I; no Catholic; Church of England oath; no foreign wars without Parliament; English-born officeholders; judges good behaviour; impeachment not pardoned
    • Act of Union 1707: Great Britain; one Parliament; free trade; uniform taxes; £398,085 compensation; Presbyterian Church; Scottish law; Union flag
  • 19th-Century Timeline
    • Reform: census 1801; slave trade 1807; Catholic Emancipation 1829; Great Reform 1832; slavery abolished 1833; bull baiting banned 1835; mines 1842; 10-hour 1847; secret ballot 1872; Reform 1867/1884
    • Monarchy: George IV 1820; William IV 1830; Victoria 1837; Albert 1840; Albert dies 1861; jubilees 1887/1897
    • Industry/science: Davy lamp 1815; railway 1825; Bessemer 1856; HMS Warrior 1860; underground 1863; Lister 1865; Swan/Edison 1878–79; electric streetlight 1881; electric underground 1890; Darwin 1859
    • Society: Peterloo 1819; Christmas Carol 1843; Health Act 1848; Great Exhibition 1851; Crimean 1854–56; Indian Mutiny 1857–58; Education Act 1870; Bank Holiday 1871; Public Health 1872/75; chimney boys banned 1875
  • 19th-Century Life
    • Population: 9 mln (1801) → 41 mln (1901); first industrial/urban society; 50%+ urban by 1851; 15 mln emigrated 1815–1914; Irish famine 1840s; Russian Jews 1880s
    • Classes: oligarchy → reform; 80% working class; middle class = at least one servant; father head; woman’s property to husband until 1882; divorce 1857 rare
    • Religion: 1851 — 40% at church; 1881 — 1/3; decline
    • Housing: back-to-backs; cellar dwellings banned 1840s; through houses; 1875 building regs; 1880s 2 up/2 down + garden; indoor toilet late 19th c.; scullery/copper/mangle; grange; gaslight 1840s–1870s; gas fires 1880s; gas cookers 1890s; bathrooms 1870s–80s; Art Nouveau 1890s
    • Food: bread/butter/potatoes/bacon → varied; cheap grain/meat; fish & chips 1860s; tinned food 1880s; margarine 1870s; ketchup 1874; chocolate bar 1847; milk chocolate 1875
    • Leisure: Bank Holiday 1871; weekend 1890s; football rules 1863; England–Scotland 1872; Queensberry 1867; AAA 1880; rugby 1823; lawn tennis 1873; snooker 1875; volleyball 1895; bicycle 1885/pneumatic 1892; photography 1835/1888; parks; department store 1863; modern Christmas
    • Education: church schools; grants 1833; dame schools; Forster 1870; compulsory 1880 (5–10); free 1891; until 12 from 1899; public schools; grammar; private girls; savage discipline; dunce cap
    • Clothes: trousers, waistcoats, coats; top/bowler/cloth caps; crinolines → bustle; corsets; drawers 1800 → knickers; children’s clothes; safety pin 1849; dry cleaning 1855; zip 1893; paper pattern 1863
  • 19th-Century Documents
    • Act of Union 1800: UK of Great Britain and Ireland; Dublin Parliament abolished; 100 MPs; Anglican Church; free trade; separate Exchequer (2/17); no Catholic office; no emancipation; Union Jack with St Patrick
    • People’s Charter 1838: vote for all men 21+; secret ballot; equal districts; no property qualification; payment of MPs; annual Parliaments; 1.3 mln signatures; rejected 235–46; Newport 1839; 1848 last burst; 5 of 6 achieved
    • India Proclamation 1858: EIC offices confirmed; treaties maintained; no territorial extension; native princes respected; impartial admission to office; ancient rights/customs; clemency except murderers of British subjects
  • Queen Victoria (1819–1901)
    • Life: born 1819; queen 1837; married Albert 1840; 9 children; Albert dies 1861; mourning; Empress of India 1876; jubilees 1887/1897; died 1901
    • Role: constitutional monarchy; Albert’s influence; conservative tinge; withdrawal → unpopularity → return; “Victorian” ethics

Seminar 8 — General Outline of the UK

  • Geography & Resources
    • Composition: England, Wales, Scotland, N. Ireland; GB = Eng+Wales+Sco; 900 km N–S; 500–600 km E–W; ≤120 km from sea
    • Relief: mostly plain; England lowland + 6 hilly regions (Pennines, Lake District, Yorkshire Dales, Cornwall/Devon moors, borders); Wales mountainous; Scotland = Highlands/Lowlands/Uplands; N. Ireland plain + uplands; Ben Nevis, Snowdon
    • Lakes/rivers: Lough Neagh, Loch Erne, Lomond, Ness; Severn, Thames, Trent
    • Climate: mild/damp; Gulf Stream; rain Apr/Oct; Lake District wettest; +5…+16°C
    • Seas: English Channel (S); North Sea (E); Irish Sea + Atlantic (W)
    • Resources: coal, oil, gas, limestone, chalk, gypsum, silica, salt, china clay, iron, tin, silver, gold, lead; agriculture 60% food needs, 1% labour, 2% GDP; land: arable 25%, pasture 46%, forest 10%; wave/tidal potential
  • BOTs & Crown Dependencies
    • BOTs: 14 territories; not part of UK; King + governor; own laws/courts; ~200k pop.; Anguilla, Bermuda, BAT, BIOT, BVI, Cayman, Akrotiri/Dhekelia, Falklands, Gibraltar, Montserrat, Pitcairn, St Helena (+Ascension, Tristan), S. Georgia/S. Sandwich, Turks & Caicos; tourism/finance/stamps
    • Crown Dependencies: Isle of Man, Jersey, Guernsey; King + Lieutenant-Governor; own parliament; UK handles defence/foreign affairs; UK no power without consent
    • Isle of Man: Irish Sea; 572 km²; Snaefell 621 m; Calf of Man, St Patrick’s, St Michael’s
    • Jersey: off France; Bailiff; States of Jersey; St Helier; 97k+
    • Guernsey: off Normandy; 65,573; Guernésiais 2% speak
  • National Parks (15)
    • General: populated; private land; farms/villages/heritage; oldest Peak District 1951; newest South Downs 2009
    • Cairngorms: largest 1,467 sq mi; 5 of 6 highest; 17k people
    • Loch Lomond/Trossachs: 720 sq mi; 70% Scots within 1 hr
    • Northumberland: 405 sq mi; 2,200 people; Hadrian’s Wall–Cheviots; 10k yrs habitation
    • Lake District: 885 sq mi; 4 peaks >3,000 ft; 50 lakes; Wordsworth/Coleridge/de Quincey
    • Yorkshire Dales: 680 sq mi; U-shaped valleys; walking
    • North York Moors: heather/peat/bogs; Rievaulx; Whitby (664; Dracula); Brontë
    • Peak District: 555 sq mi; oldest; dales
    • Snowdonia: 840 sq mi; Snowdon 3,560 ft; Cader Idris
    • Pembrokeshire Coast: 240 sq mi; 260 mi coast; only coastal NP
    • Brecon Beacons: mesas/bluffs; dolmens/standing stones
    • Norfolk Broads: 117 sq mi; 125 mi waterways; man-made medieval
    • Exmoor: 267 sq mi; highest sea cliffs; ponies; red deer
    • Dartmoor: 450 mi paths; ponies; Hound of the Baskervilles; military range
    • New Forest: 1079 William; Domesday; lowland heath; Knightwood Oak 500 yrs; ponies/agisters/drifts
    • South Downs: 2009; chalk; 99-mi Way
  • Demography
    • Population: 68 mln+; England 84.3%, Scotland 8.1%, Wales 4.7%, NI 2.8%
    • Age: 0–14 16.91%; 15–64 64.03%; 65+ 19.06%; median 40.6; life exp. 82.05 (m 80.01, f 84.21); fertility 1.63; birth rate 10.8; death 9.12; growth 0.49%
    • Ethnicity: white 87.2%; black 3%; Indian 2.3%; Pakistani 1.9%; mixed 2%; other 3.7%; 2 mln+ Afro-Caribbean/Asian since 1950s
    • Languages: English; Scots 30%; Gaelic 60k; Welsh 20%; Irish 10%; Cornish 2–3k
    • Religion: Christian 59.5%; Muslim 4.4%; Hindu 1.3%; other 2%; unspecified 7.2%; none 25.7%; C of E + Kirk established; free churches; Catholics 1950; Jews 17th c.; Hindus/Sikhs/Buddhists/Muslims
    • Urban: 83.1%; London 10.313 m; Manchester 2.646 m; Birmingham 2.515 m; Glasgow 1.223 m
  • Stereotypes
    • Queuing; hate foreigners; Queen’s English; rain daily; tea all day (1.89 kg/yr, 6th); class obsession; royal celebrations (24.5 m viewers); bad food; rude; pubs; dislike strangers; “Sorry”; bad at languages; rule-following; weather talk
  • Cities

Seminar 9 — Political System of the UK

  • Conservative Party
    • Official: Conservative and Unionist Party; centre-right; conservatism + Unionism
    • Leader: Kemi Badenoch (2 Nov 2024, unopposed)
    • Organised 1830s; Locke on government/property; property protects against totalitarianism
    • No permanent programme; pre-election manifesto
    • Support: aristocracy, farmers, small-town businessmen, working-class social reformers
  • Labour Party
    • 1900; trade unions + social organisations; Labour Representation Committee; 1906 Labour Party
    • Post-WWI socialism; nationalisation; equality; today social-democratic reformism; centre-left
    • Leader: Keir Starmer
    • Structure: individual members, CLPs, affiliated unions, socialist societies, Co-op Party; PLP; NEC, Conference, NPF; leadership final say
  • SNP
    • Nationalist; Scottish independence within EU; formed 1934 (NPS 1928 + Scottish Party 1932)
    • Internal divisions; WWII controversy; 1950 Stone of Scone; 1974 breakthrough (30%+, 11/71 seats); 20–25% polls
    • Leader: John Swinney; accepts devolution; moderate/left of centre; anti-Conservative; interventionist; non-nuclear
  • Liberal Democrats
    • 1988 merger (Liberal + SDP 1981); centre-left, libertarian
    • Leader: Sir Ed Davey (2020, 63.5%)
    • Radical/social liberalism; constitutional reform; electoral reform; devolution; Lords reform; FOI; bill of rights; pro-EU
    • Federal: England/Scotland/Wales; regional splits; semi-autonomous parliamentary parties
  • UKIP
    • Key policy: leave EU; broadened appeal (immigration, tax, grammar schools, anti-gay marriage)
    • Roots: Anti-Federalist League (Alan Sked, 1991 Maastricht); founded 1993
    • Interim leader: Neil Hamilton; Deputy: Pat Mountain; right-wing; radical right
    • Leader + NEC; 12 regional groups + Gibraltar; nationalist core
  • County Councils
    • Most important local unit; industrial planning, roads, transport, police, fire, education, social care, libraries, waste, trading standards
    • Full council decides; work delegated to groups/officers; publish decisions/papers/minutes (5 days)
    • Civic mayor (ceremonial) vs elected mayor (day-to-day); 4-year terms; first Thursday in May; all/half/third elected; declare interests
  • District Councils
    • 60–100k per district; housing, rubbish, recycling, Council Tax, planning, minor
    • Part-time unpaid councillors; full-time specialists; some called boroughs
    • 326 districts: 36 metropolitan, 32 London, 201 non-metropolitan, 55 unitary + City of London + Isles of Scilly
  • Trade Unions
    • ~7.5 mln members; TUC 1868; not in NI; ICTU 218,514 in NI
    • Unite (2007, Amicus+T&G; 200k+ public); UNISON (1.3 mln; public); GMB (620k; general/manual)
    • Three = 56% TUC; two = 46%
    • ~50% TUC affiliated to Labour; 60%+ Labour income; less formal influence now
